The instructional cycle

Plan → Build → Study → Revise

Start with the goals and materials you already have. Use technology to move, organize, present, assess, and revise them faster.

PLAN

What are students expected to learn or experience? What existing materials will carry that work? What evidence will help you decide what comes next?

BUILD

Move the materials you already have into the form students need: Canvas Pages / Modules, paper resources, quizzes, or a coordinated digital + paper experience.

STUDY

Collect and compare student performance with student experience. Look for what is working, what needs to return, and what should shape the next lesson.

REVISE

Use that evidence to change, reformat, re-embed, reorganize, or carry forward the materials students will encounter next.

Forward-looking feedback: 1.1 → evidence → stronger 1.2 → evidence → stronger 1.3. Sometimes you revise the same resource; often you use what you learned to shape the next one.
AI rule for this workshop: bring the instructional content. AI helps move, organize, convert, format, compare, and revise it. It does not decide what students should learn or invent the course for you.
Why this structure?

The fundamentals have not changed

Good teaching still depends on knowing what we want students to learn, creating strong learning experiences, checking what students are actually learning and experiencing, and responding to the evidence.

Use the cycle as a guide, not a required route. When you finish a product, the site will suggest a few sensible next moves. You can follow one, return to the continuum, or stop for today.

You already recognize the work

The workflow above is not a new teaching initiative. It is a more visible, more actionable version of a familiar habit: build, check, respond, and try the next version.

The framework is simple: Plan → Build → Study → Revise keeps the focus on the work teachers already do.

What technology can change

Canvas, AI, Apple tools, Newline boards, and New Quizzes can reduce the time between noticing a need and putting the next useful version in front of students.

The tools support the cycle. They do not replace it.

Continuous improvement

Plan → Build → Study → Revise

The cycle is a shared mental model for organizing the work: plan the learning, build from existing materials, study the evidence, and revise what comes next.

BLUE | PLAN

What do we want students to learn and experience?

Clarify learning, culture, capacity, independence, support, engagement, and the evidence that would help us know how things are going.

YELLOW | BUILD

Build it and put it in front of students.

Canvas, paper, AI-assisted production, QTI 2.1, alternate quiz versions, classroom technology, and the actual teaching experience.

GREEN | STUDY

What are students telling and showing us?

Friday Forms, ungraded formative evidence, student work, quiz results, comments, observation, and cultural feedback.

RED | REVISE

What do we carry into the next version?

Keep what is working, re-embed what needs another pass, change a delivery decision, revise a page or packet, move an existing alternate / mastery version into Canvas, close the loop with students, and begin the next cycle.

Culture and performance both matter. We can use formative evidence to stay accountable to high standards for learning while also checking whether students are experiencing the classroom culture and support we intended.
From the framework to the work

Use the cycle wherever it helps

PLAN is built into every pathway: what are you trying to accomplish, what are you starting with, and what evidence would matter? From there, choose a place to build, study evidence, or respond.

PLAN

Clarify what you want students to learn or experience and what would help you know how it is going.

BUILD

Create or organize the material students will actually use.

STUDY

Collect learning evidence and student experience feedback.

REVISE

Keep, tailor, re-embed, revise, reassess, or ask another question.

The cycle is continuous. Collect evidence → tailor what students experience → try it → check whether it worked → collect new evidence → repeat.

Culture + performance

Friday Forms and other checks can combine student experience evidence with ungraded formative learning evidence. Both help us stay accountable to the classroom culture and learning standards we intended.

Technology supports the fundamentals

Canvas, AI, classroom tools, and New Quizzes can make it faster to build, respond, and put the next useful version in front of students. The tools do not replace the cycle.
